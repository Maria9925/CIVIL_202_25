Basic fluid properties#

Question 1 (*)#

If the specific volume of a gas is 0.70 m3 /kg, what is its specific weight?

Answer

\(14.01 \; N/m^3\)

Hint

Specific weight (\(\gamma\)) is the weight per unit volume \(\gamma=\rho \cdot g\)

Specific volume (\(v\)) is the inverse of the density \(v=\frac{1}{\rho}\)

Click here for a video explaining the problem

Solution

Show code cell outputs Hide code cell outputs
\[\begin{split} \begin{aligned} v &= 0.70 \; \;\textrm{(m3/kg)} \\[10pt] \rho &= \frac{ 1 }{ v } \\&= \frac{ 1 }{ 0.70 } \\&= 1.43 \; \;\textrm{(kg/m3)}\\[10pt] \\[10pt] \gamma &= \rho \cdot g \\&= 1.43 \cdot 9.81 \\&= 14.01 \; \;\textrm{(N/m3)}\\[10pt] \end{aligned} \end{split}\]

Question 2 (*)[R]#

A reservoir of glycerin has a mass of 1200 kg and a volume of 0.952 m3 . Find the glycerin’s weight, mass density, specific weight and specific gravity.

Answer

\(11.77 \; kN \;; 1261 \; kg/m^3 \; ; 12.36 \; N/m^3 \; ; 1.26\)

Hint

The weight is given by the equation: \(W= m \cdot g\)

Mass density (\(\rho\)) is the mass divided by the volume \(\rho=\frac{m}{Vol}\)

Specific weight (\(\gamma\)) is the weight per unit volume and can be calculated as \(\gamma=\rho \cdot g\)

Specific gravity (\(s\)) is the ratio of the fluid’s density to the density of water at 4°C (i.e \(1000 kg/m^3\)):

\(s= \frac {\rho_{fluid}}{\rho_{water_{4c}}}\)

Click here for a video explaining the problem

Solution

Show code cell outputs Hide code cell outputs
\[\begin{split} \begin{aligned} m &= 1200 \; \;\textrm{(kg)} \\[10pt] \mathrm{Vol} &= 0.95 \; \;\textrm{(m3)} \\[10pt] W &= m \cdot g \\&= 1200 \cdot 9.81 \\&= 11772.00 \; \;\textrm{(N)}\\[10pt] \\[10pt] \rho_{glycerin} &= \frac{ m }{ \mathrm{Vol} } \\&= \frac{ 1200 }{ 0.95 } \\&= 1260.50 \; \;\textrm{(kg/m3)}\\[10pt] \\[10pt] \gamma_{glycerin} &= \rho_{glycerin} \cdot g \\&= 1260.50 \cdot 9.81 \\&= 12365.55 \; \;\textrm{(N/m3)}\\[10pt] \\[10pt] s_{glycerin} &= \frac{ \rho_{glycerin} }{ \rho_{water} } \\&= \frac{ 1260.50 }{ 1000 } \\&= 1.26 \\[10pt] \end{aligned} \end{split}\]

\[\rho = \rho_{{water}} \cdot s\]

Specific weight (\(\gamma\)) is the weight per unit volume and can be calculated as \(\gamma=\rho \cdot g\)

The volumetric flow rate is the volume of fluid passing through a given section per unit of time. The equation used is: \(Q= \frac {Vol}{t}\)

Click here for a video explaining the problem

Solution

Show code cell outputs Hide code cell outputs

Question 7 (*)[R]#

Calculate the specific weight, density and specific volume of air at 50 °C and absolute pressure of 3.4 bar. Given: Rair = 287 m2/(s2.K).

Answer

\( 36 \; N/m^3 \; ; 3.67 \; kg/m^3 \; ; 0.27 \; m^3/kg \)

Hint

By using the ideal gas law, the density of air (\(\rho\)) can be calculated as:

\[\rho = \frac{p}{R_{air} \cdot T}\]

Click here for a video explaining the problem

Solution

Show code cell outputs

The values of \(R_{air}\), \(T\), and \(p\) are given. We need to convert the temperature units to K and the pressure units to Pa, as required by the ideal gas law.

By using the ideal gas law, the density of air (\(\rho\)) can be calculated as:

\[\rho = \frac{p}{R_{air} \cdot T}\]

where:

  • \(p\) is the absolute pressure of the air

  • \(R_{air}\) is the specific gas constant for air

  • \(T\) is the absolute temperature of the air in Kelvin

Once the density is known, the specific weight (\(\gamma\)) can be calculated using the equation \(\gamma = \rho \cdot g\)

Finally, the specific volume is the inverse of the density \(v =\frac{1}{\rho}\)

Show code cell outputs Hide code cell outputs
\[\begin{split} \begin{aligned} T &= 50 + 273.15 &= 323.15 \; \;\textrm{(K)} \\[10pt] p &= 3.4 \cdot \left( 10 \right) ^{ 5 } &= 340000.00 \; \;\textrm{(Pa)} \\[10pt] R_{air} &= 287 \; \;\textrm{(m2/(s2.k))} \\[10pt] \rho &= \frac{ p }{ R_{air} \cdot T } \\&= \frac{ 340000.00 }{ 287 \cdot 323.15 } \\&= 3.67 \; \;\textrm{(kg/m3)}\\[10pt] \\[10pt] \gamma &= \rho \cdot g \\&= 3.67 \cdot 9.81 \\&= 35.96 \; \;\textrm{(N/m3)}\\[10pt] \\[10pt] v &= \frac{ 1 }{ \rho } \\&= \frac{ 1 }{ 3.67 } \\&= 0.27 \; \;\textrm{(m3/kg)}\\[10pt] \end{aligned} \end{split}\]

What pressure in MPa must be applied to water to reduce its volume by 3% under standard sea level atmospheric conditions?

\(64.2 \; MPa\)

Hint

\[\Delta p =-E_v \frac{\Delta v}{v}\]

The bulk modulus \(E_v\) can be found in a table of the physical properties of common liquids at standard sea-level atmospheric conditions.

Click here for a video explaining the problem

Solution

Show code cell outputs

The compressibility of liquids is described by the following equation:

\[\Delta p =-E_v \frac{\Delta v}{v}\]

Where:

  • \(\Delta p\) is the change in pressure

  • \(E_v\) is the bulk modulus of elasticity

  • \(\Delta v\) is the change in the specific volume

  • \(v\) is the original specific volume

Specific volume (\(v\)) can be calculated with the following equation:

\[ v= \frac{1}{\rho}= Vol/m \]

From the ICAO table, the physical properties of water at standard sea-level atmospheric pressure \(E_v\) at T=288.15 K (T=15°C) is 2.14x109 Pa.

Show code cell outputs Hide code cell outputs
\[\begin{split} \begin{aligned} \Delta_{Vol} &= \frac{ 3 }{ 100 } &= 0.03 \\[10pt] T &= 15 + 273.15 &= 288.15 \; \;\textrm{(K)} \\[10pt] E_{v} &= 2140000000.00 \; \;\textrm{(Pa)} \\[10pt] \Delta_{p} &= \left( - E_{v} \right) \cdot \left( - \Delta_{Vol} \right) \\&= \left( - 2140000000.00 \right) \cdot \left( - 0.03 \right) \\&= 64200000.00 \; \;\textrm{(Pa)}\\[10pt] \\[10pt] \Delta_{p_{}} &= \frac{ \Delta_{p} }{ 1000000 } \\&= \frac{ 64200000.00 }{ 1000000 } \\&= 64.20 \; \;\textrm{(MPa)}\\[10pt] \end{aligned} \end{split}\]

Question 13 (***)#

A rigid cylinder with inside diameter of 15 mm contains a column of water 500 mm long. What will the column length be if a force of 2 kN is applied to its top by a frictionless plunger? Assume a bulk modulus of 2.21 GPa.

Answer

\(497 \; mm\)

Hint

The compressibility of liquids is described by the following equation:

\[\Delta p =-E_v \frac{\Delta v}{v}\]

The change in pressure can be calculated using the force and area as:

\[ \Delta p = \frac {\Delta F}{A}\]

The cylinder’s cross-sectional area is fixed, but the column length is changing, modifying the fluid’s volume

Click here for a video explaining the problem

Solution

Show code cell outputs

The compressibility of liquids is described by the following equation:

\[\begin{split}\Delta p =-E_v \frac{\Delta v}{v}\\ \end{split}\]

where:

  • \(\Delta p\) is the change in pressure

  • \(E_v\) is the bulk modulus of elasticity

  • \(\Delta v\) is the change in the specific volume

  • \(v\) is the original specific volume

The change in pressure can be calculated using the force and area as:

\[\Delta p = \frac {\Delta F}{A}\]

Changing specific volume with the following equation, we have

\[\frac {\Delta v }{v}= \frac {\frac{\Delta Vol}{m}}{\frac{Vol}{m}}\]

Knowing that the change in volume is due to the change in length since the area remains constant, we have the following:

\[ \frac {\Delta v }{v}= \frac{L_2-L_1}{L_1} \]

Substituting into the compressibility equation, we have:

\[\Delta p =-E_v \frac{L_2-L_1}{L_1}\]

Solving for \(L_2\):

\[L_2 = -L_1 \cdot \frac{\Delta p}{E_v}+ L_1\]
Show code cell outputs Hide code cell outputs
\[\begin{split} \begin{aligned} \mathrm{dF} &= 2000 \; \;\textrm{(N)} \\[10pt] d &= 0.0150 \; \;\textrm{(m)} \\[10pt] L_{1} &= 0.5000 \; \;\textrm{(m)} \\[10pt] E_{v} &= 2.21 \cdot \left( 10 \right) ^{ 9 } &= 2210000000.0000 \; \;\textrm{(Pa)} \\[10pt] A &= \pi \cdot \left( \frac{ d }{ 2 } \right) ^{ 2 } \\&= 3.1416 \cdot \left( \frac{ 0.0150 }{ 2 } \right) ^{ 2 } \\&= 0.0002 \; \;\textrm{(m2)}\\[10pt] \\[10pt] \mathrm{dp} &= \frac{ \mathrm{dF} }{ A } \\&= \frac{ 2000 }{ 0.0002 } \\&= 11317684.8421 \; \;\textrm{(Pa)}\\[10pt] \\[10pt] L_{2} &= \left( - L_{1} \right) \cdot \frac{ \mathrm{dp} }{ E_{v} } + L_{1} \\&= \left( - 0.5000 \right) \cdot \frac{ 11317684.8421 }{ 2210000000.0000 } + 0.5000 \\&= 0.4974 \; \;\textrm{(m)}\\[10pt] \\[10pt] L_{2_{}} &= 497.4394 \; \;\textrm{(mm)} \end{aligned} \end{split}\]

Vapour Pressure#

Question 1 (*)[R]#

At approximately what temperature will water boil at top of Mount Everest (elevation 8 850 m above sea level)?

Answer

\(70 \; °C\)

Hint

Click here for a video explaining the problem

Solution
