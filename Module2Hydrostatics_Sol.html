Question 1 (*)#

A square block has a contact area of 0.02 m2 with a table that is lubricated with a 0.1 mm thick layer of oil with viscosity 7 mPa.s. What force is required to move the block at a constant velocity of 1 m/s?

Answer

\(1.4 \; N\)

Hint

Since the block moves at constant velocity, the net forces in this problem is zero:

\[\begin{split} F-\tau A=0\\ \end{split}\]
\[ \tau = \mu \frac {\Delta V}{\Delta y} \]

Solution

Show code cell outputs

Since the block moves at constant velocity, the net forces in this problem is zero:

\[\begin{split} \sum F=0\\ \end{split}\]

this means the applied force must balance the force caused by the oil’s viscosity:

\[\begin{split} F-\tau A=0\\ \end{split}\]

where:

  • \(F\) is the force required to move the block.

  • \(A\) is the contact area.

  • \(\tau\) is the shear stress, which according to Newton´s viscosity law, can be expressed as the absolute viscosity (\(\mu\)) multiplied by the rate of change of velocity (\(\frac {\Delta V}{\Delta y}\)):

\[ \tau = \mu \frac {\Delta V}{\Delta y} \]

\(5.16 \; m/s\)

\[\begin{split} F_\mu=\tau A\\ \end{split}\]

\[\begin{split} \sum F_x=0\\ \end{split}\]

\[\begin{split} \mu \frac{dV}{dy} A=Wsin\theta\\ \end{split}\]
